Key terms
Close down
Move toward the ball to take space away from the shooter.
Set
Stopped, balanced, and in the right shape before the ball is struck. Not moving at the strike.
Set position
The body shape a keeper takes when getting set. See the three shapes below.
Positioning
Where the keeper stands: across the goal and how far off the line. Not the same as the set position.
Angle
The space between two lines running from the ball to each post. Stand in the middle of it to cover the most goal. Closing down shrinks it.
Through ball
A pass into the space behind the defenders.
50/50
A ball the keeper and attacker would reach at the same time.
ReferenceThe set shapes, by distanceStandard, low, super-low, higher. Tap to open.

The closer the ball, the less time there is, so the keeper gets lower. Every keeper's set is a little different (hands, height, gap between the legs). Keep the principles and adjust to what feels comfortable.

WhenFeet and legsBodyHands
Standardfrom distance Closing down, and shots from distance or the edge of the box Shoulder width. Weight on the soles toward the toes, not flat-footed. Knees slightly bent Chest over the knees. Head still, eyes on the ball In front, around waist height, palms toward the ball
Lowclose range Stopped just in front of the ball. Close range or along the ground Same base, knees bent more. Legs closed so nothing goes through Wide but closed, square to the ball, big body behind it Out to the sides to widen the barrier, palms toward the ball, low enough to cover the ground
Super-low1v1 Closing down an attacker in a 1v1, very close Even lower. Legs closer together than a regular set Even lower than the low set Close to the ground, open palms to the ball to fill the goal frame
Higheraerial balls Chest to head height shots Stand taller, knees still slightly bent Wide but closed Adjust to the height of the ball

Standard base: too narrow makes it hard to dive, too wide makes it hard to adjust the feet. Too straight in the legs and you cannot generate power quickly, too bent and you are too low to move.

Find the hand position: catch a ball in front of the face, lower it to the belly, drop it, and leave the hands where they are. From there the hands are an equal distance from high, low and wide shots.

Why lower up close: less reaction time, and the lower the body, the faster it gets to low shots.

Warm-up: Hands, Feet, Reaction

Pairs · goal line to the 18
20′
2 keepers1 ball each, then partner work
Dribble to the 18
Dribble from the goal line to the edge of the 18. Both feet, quick touches, ball under control.
Grass touches
Move forward with hands out front in keeper position. Reach down and touch the grass left, then right, staying low and balanced.
Ball drops
Walk forward, drop the ball onto your foot and catch it with both hands. Repeat every couple of steps. On the way back, bounce the ball off alternating knees.
Lateral tosses
Partners face each other and side-step to the edge of the 18 and back, tossing the ball between them. Stay low with hands ready.
Forwards and backwards tosses
One keeper runs backwards, the other forwards, tossing the ball between them to the 18. Reverse direction and come back.
Overhead throws
Throw the ball overhead, straight into the ground. Catch it and repeat on the other side, alternating each throw.
Side step toss and catch
Partners stand about four feet apart, slightly offset, each holding a ball. Both toss, side-step, and catch the other's ball. Repeat for 30 seconds.
Dynamic stretch
Finish with your usual dynamic stretch (leg swings, walking lunges, arm circles).
Look for: low and balanced, hands active, quick feet, eyes on the ball.

Set: Body Positioning and Shot Block

Close down, set, block
15′
Close down a step or two, stop just in front of the ball, and set before the strike. The closer the keeper gets, the less of the goal the shooter can see.
Coach shootsKeeper starts a step or two back from the ballBall on the goal area line

Setup

  • Ball on the goal area line, coach a step back from it.
  • Keeper starts a step or two back from the ball, centered on it.
  • Balls stacked beside the coach. The resting keeper retrieves balls and watches.
  • 5 to 6 reps at each stage, then swap keepers.

Run it

  1. Fake. Coach pretends to shoot, no strike. Keeper closes down and sets in the standard set, then low set at the ball. A few reps to lock in the shape.
  2. Light shots. Coach takes light shots from the goal area line. Keeper closes, sets, and blocks.
  3. Ball out. Move the ball out a few yards and strike it for real. The keeper has farther to close and must time the set to the strike.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • Start a step or two back, finish just in front of the ball.Where did you start, and where did you stop?
  • Set before the strike. Slightly off the ideal spot but set beats perfect and still moving.Were your feet set when I struck it?
  • Wide and closed, body behind the ball.Could the ball get under your hands or through your legs?
  • Big body.How much of the goal could I see?
Shapes for this activity: standard and low set.
2

Move: Low Angle Shot Block with Ladder

Footwork, then set, then block
15′
Add movement to the shape. Small steps keep the head level and the eyes steady, then the keeper sets and blocks the low angle shot.
Coach and resting keeper serveLadder on the goal lineBoth sides, then swap

Setup

  • Ladder, poles or cones to step over, along the goal line.
  • Coach on one side, resting keeper on the other, both at the goal area line with balls.
  • After the last step, keeper takes one or two small steps off the line toward the shooter, then sets.

Run it

  1. Through the steps. Any footwork works: side steps, forward, backward. The steps stay small and the head and eyes stay level.
  2. Set and block. Keeper sets and blocks the one-touch low angle shot from the near server.
  3. Back and forth. Back through to the other side, next shot. Several passes each way, then swap.

Then raise the shot

  1. Ground. Server strikes low along the ground. Keeper sets in the low set.
  2. Knee to waist. Server tosses the ball up and volleys it. The hands and body adjust to the height. Stay wide but closed.
  3. Chest to head. Same toss and volley. Same footwork. Higher set: stand taller, knees still slightly bent.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • Small steps, head level. A bobbing head loses depth perception and makes the ball harder to track.Was your head bobbing? Could you see the ball the whole way? Did your eyes stay steady while your feet moved?
  • Set at the strike.Were you moving or set when the ball was hit?
  • Cover the post nearest the ball first so nothing sneaks inside, then angle across to close the rest of the goal. Do not hug the post.Could anything have slipped inside the post? How much of the rest of the goal could the shooter see?
  • Recover fast.How quickly were you ready for the next side?

Decide: Recover and 1v1

Through balls, recovering defenders, keeper decides and talks
30′
Put the shape and footwork into a live decision. The keeper reads the through ball, decides to win it or close down and set, and tells the defender what is coming.
2 keepers + coachAssistant coach passes4 to 6 field playersKeeper starts at the edge of the goal area

Setup

  • Keeper starts at the edge of the goal area, not on the line, so there is room to come out.
  • Three cones in a line out near midfield: left, center, right. They are the starting places and gates, and the extra distance gives the keeper time to read the play.
  • Attacker starts at the center cone and makes a lateral run to a side cone to get up to speed, then breaks for the through ball.
  • Defender starts at the opposite side cone, runs laterally around the center cone, then recovers. This adds time before the chase.
  • Switch sides to work both. Move the cones farther out or closer in as needed: observe and adapt.
  • The assistant coach plays the through balls so the coach can work with the keepers. Rotate field players in pairs.

StageWhat changesCalls added

4Last 8 to 10 minutes: 2v1 with a recovering defenderAll

Keeper calls: if, then

IfYellThen
You can get to the ball first"Keeper!"Go get it early.
It is a 50/50"Keeper!" or "Chase!"Decide in the first step and fully commit: go and call Keeper, or stay, set, and call Chase. No half-commits.
Attacker gets there first and the defender is close"Chase!"Commits your defender to pressure on the ball. Close down and set behind that pressure.
Attacker gets there first and the ball is wide"Inside!"Defender closes the angle and covers the lane. Set early and narrow the angle.
Defender gets there first"Away!"Defender clears it away from goal. Keeper recovers to goal.
Vary the pass (coach calls it)
  • Distance: short, 50/50, long
  • Speed: slow, firm, bouncing
  • Angle: central or wide
  • Chase gap: tight or far
  • Passer's head: down under pressure, or up with time, before the pass
Rotation and field players
  • Keepers swap in goal every 5 to 6 reps, about 15 minutes in goal each. The resting keeper retrieves balls and watches, and can call out what they see.
  • Tell the field players: defenders recover at full speed. Attackers choose: dribble, shoot, or chip. The keeper's decision decides the outcome.
  • Safety: no studs up, no late challenges on the keeper.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • Read the passer, then the ball, then the attacker, then the chaser. Head down under pressure means a good ball is hard to play, so stay patient. Head up with time means be ready for the ball in behind. If a body blocks the ball, adjust slightly until you can see it.What did you see first, the ball, the attacker, or the chaser? Could you see the ball the whole way?
  • Commit fully or set early.What was your trigger to come out or stay, and what did you yell?
  • Talk early and loud.Did your defender do what you wanted? Why or why not?
  • Standard set as you come out, lower as you get closer: low set, then super-low set in the 1v1. Recover to goal after any clearance.After a goal or a miss: was it the read, the call, or the commitment that broke down?
Ask before a rep: Is the ball central or wide, and what does that change? What is your call if the defender is tight? What if he is far?
Shapes for this activity: standard, low and super-low set.

Finish: Keeper Wars

Keeper v keeper, live
10′
2 keepersTwo goals, 20 yards apart

How it runs

  • Two goals 20 yards apart, one keeper in each.
  • One keeper shoots, the other saves, swap after each shot.
  • The shooter can use any service: kick off the ground, volley, half volley, or throw.
  • One point per goal. Two 3-minute halves, most goals wins.
  • Coach keeps balls coming so play never stops.
Coach it
  • Apply the angles and body shape live.
  • Get set as the ball is struck.
  • Recover quickly after every shot.
  • Keep it fun and competitive.
