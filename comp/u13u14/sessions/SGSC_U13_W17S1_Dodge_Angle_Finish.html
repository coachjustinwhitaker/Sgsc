Equipment

Key terms
Dead stop
Stop the ball so it does not move.
Drag
Pull the ball sideways or back with the sole of the foot. A triple drag is three small drags in a row.
Scissor
Step a foot over the ball without touching it, then take the ball the other way with your other foot. Here it is a small angle change, not a way to beat someone.
Engage
Drive at a defender so he steps, then go past him.
Dummy
A teammate who stands in as a defender and does not tackle.
Check in, check away
Check in: step toward the passer to block the dummy. Check away: step back out and follow the ball.

Warm-up: Dynamic Warm-Up

Captain-led · no ball · self-run
10′
All playersNo equipment
Light jog
Easy jog across the working area and back to loosen up.
High knees
Run in a straight line, driving the knees up toward the waist.
Butt kicks
Run in a straight line, kicking the heels up toward the backside.
Straight-leg walks
Walk forward, swinging one straight leg up toward the opposite hand, alternating legs.
Lateral shuffle, both directions
Stay low and shuffle sideways without crossing the feet. Go one way, then back the other way.
Carioca, both directions
Travel sideways, crossing the trailing foot in front, then behind, with the hips turning. Go one way, then back the other way.
Walking lunge with rotation
Step into a lunge and rotate the upper body toward the front leg. Stand, step, and repeat on the other side.
Leg swings
Hold a teammate or stand tall. Swing one leg front to back, then side to side. Switch legs.

Touch: Dodge, Scissor, Angle, Flip

Pairs · constant pace, not a race
15′
Players string small touches together to change the angle of the ball, then drive out calm. It is an angle change, not a move to beat someone, so the ball never goes far.
7 pairs, 7 identical setupsTurf50% pace to start

Setup

  • Build the setup as drawn, and build it the same way for every pair.
  • One ball per pair. One player starts at the bottom with the ball, the other at the top by the speed square.
  • The top player steps in and out of the speed square on their toes until the dribbling player is ready to pass, then steps forward into the pass.
  • Players ask questions at any time. This is not a race, so asking costs nobody anything.

The pattern

  1. Pass. The bottom player passes up to the top player, who steps forward into the pass.
  2. Dead stop. The top player stops the ball above the speed square.
  3. Drag and stop outside. Drag the ball to the outside cone and stop it there (Croquetta).
  4. Scissor inside. Scissor and push the ball back inside with your inside foot (your left foot on the right version).
  5. Stop and flip back out. Stop it, then a drag and a flick back out.
  6. Triple drag. Three small drags. If you only get two, that is fine.
  7. Outside around the cone. Take it outside and around the bottom cone, where the passer started.

Run it

  1. Walk it (2′). Each pair walks the seven steps once, slowly, so everyone knows the order.
  2. Right version (6′). 50% pace, constant. After every rep the pair swaps ends: the receiver finishes at the bottom, the passer runs the dashed arc to the top.
  3. Switch (1′). Pairs reset the setup as a mirror image.
  4. Left version (6′). Same pattern with the whole layout flipped, so everything that went right now goes left.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • Do not go far across on the scissor. Think angle change.Where did your scissor send the ball: across, or just a new angle?
  • Correct it right away. Do not keep fading.After the flip, which way is your body facing?
  • Quick touches, then calm.What did you do to stay calm after the quick ones?
  • Ask if you are not sure.Which part do you want to see again?

Read: Check In, Check Away, Finish

Two mirrored setups · one goal
15′
The attacker checks in to block the dummy, then reads the ball. If it is light, keep stepping in. If it is really hard, take a touch. Otherwise check back out, follow the ball, and take a one-touch shot.
6 to 7 players per sideKeeper in goalBoth sides at once

Setup

  • Two setups at the edge of the box, one on each side, both shooting at the same goal.
  • Each side has a passer with a ball, an attacker and a dummy, plus a line of 3 or 4 waiting.
  • The dummy stays behind the cones, passive, and does not tackle.
  • Both sides shoot at one goal. Players look up and make sure the keeper is set before they shoot.

What the pass tells you

PassDo this
LightKeep stepping in and take it, then finish.
Really hardTake a touch first, then finish.
In betweenCheck back out, follow the ball, and take a one-touch shot.
The passer does not call the speed. Players learn to read it. Talk about the speeds as they come up.

Run it

  1. Walk it (2′). Walk one rep slowly so everyone knows their role and the rotation.
  2. Rotation. 1 passes, then becomes the dummy (2). 2 becomes the attacker (3). 3 finishes, then goes to the back of the line. The next in line becomes 1. Move quickly so the ball keeps rolling.
  3. First side (6′). Everyone works their side. The attacker finishes every rep.
  4. Switch (1′). Groups swap sides. After right, get in line for left, and the other way round.
  5. Second side (6′). Same rotation on the other side. Weak-foot reps come naturally on the mirrored side.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • Play it through. Work with whatever the pass ends up being.What did the pass weight tell you to do?
  • Make the turn a step and a pop.When did you take your step: when the ball was close enough?
  • Do not take the shooting step until you know you are on the ball.How did you know the ball was yours?
  • If the pass is really hard, take a touch.How did you know it was too hard to step into?
  • Speak up and talk to your teammate.What did you say to the passer?

Combine: One-Two, Correction Touch, Engage

Groups of four · two mirrored setups
15′
Dodge the first defender with your first touch, then engage the second and finish. This is how one player beats more than one defender.
4 per setup plus a lineKeeper in goalMirror the setup on the other side

Setup

  • Build the setup as drawn. Build a second one on the other side of the goal so all players can participate. Both sides shoot at the same goal.
  • Four players per setup, numbered 1 to 4. A line of 2 or 3 waits behind.
  • Player 2 is the dummy. The pair of cones in the box is where 4 engages.

The pattern

  1. 1 passes to 4.
  2. 4 passes to 3 and dodges their own cone with the movement.
  3. 3 plays it back into 4's run.
  4. 4 takes a correction touch, engages 2, and finishes on goal.

Run it

  1. Walk it (2′). Walk one rep slowly so everyone knows their number and the rotation.
  2. Rotation. After 4 shoots, 4 jogs to the back of the line. The next in line becomes 1, and 1 moves to 2, 2 to 3, 3 to 4.
  3. First side (6′). Everyone works their side.
  4. Switch (1′). Groups swap sides.
  5. Second side (6′). Same rotation on the mirrored side.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • First touch changes the angle. Second touch drives.What did your first touch say, and what did your second say?
  • Soft and forward. Do not go across.How far did that touch take you off your line?
  • Do not rush to play the ball. Take a touch, then engage.Why did you take that touch: a bad ball, or to control the tempo?
  • Show nothing. Stay relaxed, then pop.What did your body show the defender before you moved?
  • Once you go inside, do not keep going inside.Where did you take the space after the correction touch?

Free: Free Dribble, Shoot on Call

Everyone at once · penalty box area
6′
Players dribble freely at 50% and find space to shoot in traffic. Finding the space is the skill.
All players, one ball eachKeeper in goalShot call every 90 seconds

Setup

  • Mark an area around the penalty box with a few cones. Everyone dribbles inside it.
  • Every player has a ball. Keep spare balls by the goal.
  • The keeper is in goal. Shots are on the keeper from wherever the player is.

Run it

  1. Dribble. 50% effort, a new skill every few seconds. Use the space. Do not just dribble, find skills.
  2. Call "shoot." Every 90 seconds. Players get two touches to get a shot off from where they are. No running to a better spot.
  3. Go again. After the shot, grab a ball and dribble. Six minutes gives about four calls.
Heads up: 14 players shoot at one goal. Players dribble with their heads up and look before they strike.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • Do not decide the move. Let your feet pick it.When did you stop thinking about what to do?
  • Shoot from the outside of the traffic.Where was the space when you heard the call?
  • Two touches. The first one makes the shot.What did your first touch set up?
  • Use the space to change angles.Which way did you go when the space closed?

Play: Final Scrimmage

Half-field or whole field, depending on Blue
29′
Free play to see whether the dodges, the reads and the combinations show up in a game.
White vs White on half-field, or White vs Blue on the whole fieldOne live set-piece rep

Setup

  • If Blue is busy: White vs White, 7v7 on half the field. Pinnies in two colors. Goals are the full-size goal with the keeper at one end and a cone goal at the other end.
  • If Blue is free: White vs Blue on the whole field, with a goal at each end.
  • Free play either way. No scoring rules and no restrictions.

Run it

  1. Play. Run the scrimmage for 29 minutes.
  2. Set-piece rep. At some point, call one live set-piece rep.
Coach it: cue, then ask
  • Dodge the first defender, then engage the second.Where did you beat one defender and drive at the next?
  • Check in, check away.When did you check in to shake the defender?
  • Read the pass.What did the pass weight tell you to do?
