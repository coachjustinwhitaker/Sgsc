Philosophy Connection

This opens Peak Performance, the point in the season where you're the fittest and best trained you'll be before the break. Every block from here cycles at Application through repeated performance, not new content, so today is about composure and decisions in the moments B1 through B4 have been building toward: breaking a line, reading a defender's angle, and getting back into the play after your shot instead of standing on it.

Session Objectives
  • Recognize the moment a line-breaking pass is on, and the moment a shooting window opens under closing pressure
  • Execute a line-breaking pass into the box, a through ball, a back-to-goal lay-off, or a switch to the far side, and finish first time
  • Read the winger's jockey angle as a group, cross service or a shot, and arrive in the right zone for it
  • Build the habit of resetting onside after a shot and following the next ball in, rather than standing on the moment
  • Manage tempo and communicate the game state in the closing scrimmage against Blue

Passing Mastery Squares — V1 Back Foot / V2 3rd Man / V3 1-2 Combo / V4 Diagonal
Objective Pure technical reps on the turf before anything else today. Groups of 5 throughout, the fifth player rotates in as a periodic swap so nobody regroups between patterns. Time here is a ceiling, get through as many variations as the group can handle cleanly.
Four Variations, Corners Numbered 1–4 (Top-Left, Top-Right, Bottom-Right, Bottom-Left)
V1 · Back Foot (~2′)Ball circulates corner to corner around the square. Receive on the back foot, open the body before playing it on.
V2 · 3rd Man (~3′)1→2, 2 lays it back but only to the midpoint between 1 and 2, where 1's player meets it and redirects on to 3, not back to 2. Same pattern continues, 3→2 laid to midpoint→4, then 4→3 laid to midpoint→1. The give-and-receive never repeats between the same two players.
V3 · 1-2 Combo (~3′)A real give-and-go on each edge: 1→2, 2 lays back to midway, 1 plays it right back out to midway again, advance to the next edge using that corner as the new start. Runs 1↔2, then 2↔3, then 3↔4, then 4↔1 to close the loop.
V4 · Diagonal (~2′)Ball goes to the adjacent corner first, then diagonally across to the far corner, crossing the opposite diagonal in the middle. Tests disguised passing and run timing so paths don't collide.
Coaching Points
  • Inside-foot contact, plant foot pointed at the target
  • Weight the pass to the receiver's feet
  • Quick set touch to open the next passing angle early
  • Call for it before the ball arrives, don't receive in silence

3v2 Line-Break + Wide Finish — Representative Rep, Wingers Alternate Sides Each Rep, Puggs at the Base for Defense to Counter Into
Objective Two finishing chances off one sequence, central and wide. The pass that breaks the line is the trigger for everything else: the runner's shot, the winger's run, and the reset that follows.

Setup

Top of the box, one goal, 1 in net. 4 and 5 defend. 6, 8, 9, 10 rotate through the attacking 3. 7 and 11 are the wingers, 2 and 3 the fullbacks on their side, alternating which side goes live each rep. A coach stands in each wide lane as the passive defender.

Rules

The line-breaking action has to be a pass, no dribbling past 4 and 5 to break the line in this activity. Once the pass breaks the line, the runner takes a one-touch shot, and on the same trigger the winger on that side drives at the coach. After the shot, the shooter gets back level with 4 and 5, onside, then follows the winger's ball in.

Scoring

Attack: 1 point for a pass that breaks the line, whether or not the chance is scored. 1 point for a shot that scores. Defense: 1 point for winning the ball and scoring into the pugg goal.

Coach Note

Alternate which side goes live each rep, 7/2 one rep, 11/3 the next, so both wingers and both fullbacks get even reps. Rotate one attacker out of the 3 every rep. Second ball goes in immediately after a miss or a save, the reset is the rep.

Sets
Set 1 · Defenders Hold4 and 5 stay outside the box after the pass. Winger drives solo at the coach.
Set 2 · Defenders Chase4 and 5 release and chase the moment the line-breaking pass is played. Keeper can come off the line to close the angle. Winger and fullback work as a pair, the driver fixes the coach, the partner overlaps.
Coaching Points — The Reads
  • Line-break: can you split the defense, a grounded ball into the gap between 4 and 5, for a runner in behind?
  • Line-break: can you receive back to goal, lay it off to a teammate facing goal, and get played in yourself?
  • Line-break: if 4 and 5 get pulled to one side, can you switch the point, a ball into the space on the far side for the runner arriving there?
  • Winger: if the coach pushes you outside, get ready to cross it. If the coach pushes you inside, run in for the shot
  • Inside players: winger pushed outside, prepare for a crossing service, hold your zone, near post, far post, late arrival. Winger pushed inside, prepare for a rebound off the keeper or a low ground pass
  • Shooter: after your shot, get back onside before you do anything else, then follow the next ball in

Positional Rondo — 6 Anchors the Back, 8/10 Central, 9 Up Top, 7/11 Wide, 4/5 Defend Into the Puggs
Objective Same personnel and the same top-of-box space as Activity 3, now in a possession shape instead of a single repetition. The team has to keep the ball under real pressure, then release on the same reads once the picture opens up, a wide run or a central line-break, so the reps from Activity 3 show up live instead of on a whistle.

Setup

Same grid as Activity 3, no need to move the goal or cones. 6 serves the rondo in from outside the grid, then anchors the back of the shape. 8 and 10 sit central, 9 up top near the edge of the box, 7 and 11 wide in the lanes as outlets on both sides. 4 and 5 press inside the shape to win the ball back.

Rules

The group has to combine at least 5 passes before breaking to goal. Once that's met, they release: a ball to either winger, who runs it to shoot or cross, or a line-break to 9, who turns and finishes. The defender nearest the release sprints to recover and contest it, same chase mechanic as Set 2 in Activity 3.

Coach Note

Rotate 4 and 5 out for two of the resting attacking players every few minutes, they'll do a lot of running once the release triggers a chase. Reset with a new ball served in by 6 after a goal, a save, or a clean win by the defenders.

Coaching Points
  • Same reads as Activity 3: can you split the defense, receive back to goal and lay it off, or switch the point when they get pulled to one side
  • Don't force the release early, string the passes together until the picture is actually open
  • Winger receiving the release: beat your recovering defender before you decide cut in or cross
  • 9 receiving centrally: get your body shape ready before the ball arrives, you'll have a defender closing fast
  • Whoever gets caught upfield after a turnover, sprint back into the shape, don't jog
Activity 5 of 5  ·  Scrimmage
Game-State Scrimmage
White Only, Then Full Field vs Blue  ·  15 minutes
Phase 1
White only, half field
Phase 2
Full field vs Blue
Duration
15 min
Objective Manage tempo and stakes as the game state demands, and get a real set-piece rep in. Start artificial, a lead to protect or a goal to chase, so the composure and communication habits from today have a reason to show up.

Phase 1 — White Only

Roughly 7 minutes, half field. Start the game 1-0 up or 0-1 down with a few minutes on the clock. Coach calls the game state, protecting the lead or chasing the goal, and the team has to respond. One live set piece, corner or free kick, worked in when the moment calls for it.

Phase 2 — Full Field vs Blue

Roughly 8 minutes, both squads merge for a real full-field game. Same game-state principles carry over, with an actual opponent and a real lead or deficit to manage.

Coaching Points
  • Coach calls the game state, protecting or chasing, respond as a team the moment you hear it
  • Composure late, are your choices getting better or worse as the stakes go up
  • Communication and ownership without being prompted, that's the standard today
