Philosophy Connection

No game this weekend means real room to push physically without managing fatigue into a match. Every conditioning rep today still runs through a soccer action, sprinting, cutting, driving, finishing, not running for its own sake.

Session Objectives
  • Build aerobic tempo and repeated-sprint capacity through soccer-specific movement
  • Sharpen reactive change of direction and ball-carrying speed under a called cue
  • Finish cleanly under real fatigue, both in the shooting circuit and the Transition Game's numbers-up waves

Phase 1 — Speed & Agility Circuit (17′)
3 Lanes — Pole + Cone Reactive Cut, Mirrored Colors Both Directions

Setup

  • 3 parallel lanes
  • Two lines facing each other, 15 yards apart
  • 2 poles per lane, positioned between the lines
  • 5 yards from each starting line to the nearest pole
  • 5 yards between the two poles
  • Red and yellow cone at the base of each pole
  • Yellow = cut left, red = cut right, from the runner's own perspective

Four Phases

1 (no ball): Sprint to the first pole, react to the called color, cut and sprint through to the far line. 2 (no ball): Same first cut, continue to the second pole, go around it, sprint back to your own line. 3 (ball): Same pattern, croqueta at the first pole toward the called side, continue to the second pole, drive back to start. 4 (ball): Same as Phase 2's movement, cut at the first pole, continue to the second pole and go around it, then pass back to the next person in line.

Coaching Points
  • React to the call, don't guess early, that's the whole point
  • Low, athletic stance, keep bouncing, be ready to go
  • Clean croqueta technique even as fatigue builds
  • Full speed every sprint segment, this is conditioning, not a walkthrough
Phase 2 — Ladder, Sprint, Recovery Jog, Drive, Shoot (13′)
Two Mirrored Lanes, One Each Side of Goal

Setup

  • 2 full mirrored lanes, one left of goal, one right
  • Shooting happens from both sides across the circuit
  • Each lane, in order out from goal: agility ladder directly in front of goal
  • Then a cone marker just beyond the ladder
  • Then the coach, stationed further out with balls
  • Then a two-cone gate near the top of the box

Sequence

Agility action through the ladder, straight into a sprint from the top of the ladder to the cone marker, a real recovery jog from there to the coach, who lays a ball in front of you, receive and drive it through the gate, shoot, retrieve, jog back to the base of the ladder to rejoin the line.

Coach Note

Footwork rotates every 2 reps through the list below. Aim for 2 reps per action and 2 full sets through the rotation, but the clock is the real constraint, let it run as a continuous flow rather than chasing an exact count.

Footwork Rotation (2 Reps Each, Cycle Through)
1. Single Steps  2. Forward-and-Backs  3. Shuffles  4. Pogos  5. Side Pogos  6. Side Steps  7. High-Knee Pogos (Knees to Chest)
Coaching Points
  • Full effort through the ladder, it's easy to coast here since it's not the sprint
  • The sprint segment has to be genuinely max effort, not a jog with intent
  • Use the recovery jog as a real breather so the finish is controlled, not just fatigue-fueled
  • First touch off the lay-off sets up the drive, don't need an extra touch to gather it

Objective Train transition recognition, the moment possession changes, go, while each wave adds two fresh players and builds all the way to 6v6. Continuous both directions, so the conditioning comes from the format itself. Walk through one full cycle slowly before going live.
Wave Attack — Entry Points Widen as the Numbers Grow

Setup

  • Half field
  • Full-size goal each end, keepers in both
  • Team A lines up behind one end line
  • Team B lines up behind the opposite end line
  • Entry cones for each wave number get progressively wider, so the attack spreads across the field as it grows instead of everyone piling into the middle at 6v6
  • One ball per wave, doesn't matter which of the two entering players has it
  • Needs close to full squad numbers, reaching 6v6 takes 14 on the field before anyone's even on the bench for the final wave
  • Walk through one full cycle slowly before going live, two minutes of explanation up front saves ten minutes of confusion once it's live
Wave Structure  ·  Every Wave Adds 2, Previous Attackers Stay On As Defenders
Wave 1 · 2v GKTeam A sends 2 fresh players forward. Empty net apart from Team B's GK. After the shot or turnover, immediately into Wave 2.
Wave 2 · 2v2Team B sends 2 fresh players the other way. Team A's 2 stay on as defenders. Immediately into Wave 3.
Wave 3 · 4v2Team A adds 2 more to their 2 (now 4), attacks back. Team B's 2 stay on as defenders. Immediately into Wave 4.
Wave 4 · 4v4Team B adds 2 more to their 2 (now 4), attacks back. Team A's 4 stay on as defenders. Immediately into Wave 5.
Wave 5 · 6v4Team A adds 2 more (now 6), attacks back. Team B's 4 stay on as defenders. Immediately into Wave 6.
Wave 6 · 6v6Team B adds 2 more (now 6), attacks back. Team A's 6 stay on as defenders. Immediately into Wave 7.
Wave 7 · Anyone LeftRemaining bench players join in, split across both teams, for one final bigger wave. After the shot or turnover, quick rest and reset.
ResetQuick rest. Team B starts the next full cycle with the free run, 2v GK. Team A starts the cycle after that, alternating who gets the free run each time.
Coaching Points
  • Transition recognition: the moment possession changes, go, no pause, no look around
  • Early waves (2v GK, 2v2): quick combination beats hesitation, take it on
  • Middle waves (4v2, 4v4): find the extra player, someone's usually open
  • 6v6 and the final wave: this is a real game moment now, shape and communication matter as much as individual quality
  • Defending: delay, don't dive in, show them where you want them to go
