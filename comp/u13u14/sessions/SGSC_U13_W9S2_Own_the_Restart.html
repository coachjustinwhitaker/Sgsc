Philosophy Connection

This is a fix-it session built around attacking set plays, corners and free kicks especially. Give players a clear structure for these runs and reads, then let the reps expose what still needs sharpening.

Session Objectives
  • Install a repeatable attacking corner structure so every runner has a clear job, not an improvised scramble
  • Read the moment on free kicks in our own half and midfield: quick restart when the opponent is scattered, controlled tempo when they are set or we are protecting a lead
  • Test the attacking patterns live in a real scrimmage against Strikers Blue without live coaching intervention

Objective Two parts. Part 1 is our standard dynamic sequence, players know it and run it themselves. Part 2 adds two throw-in specific reps on top: install chest control off a short throw and a check-and-run pattern off a long throw, building the habit of an immediate, purposeful first action, not standing over the ball. This targets what went wrong in the last two games, turning the ball over right after winning a clean restart.
Part 1 — Standard Dynamics (Self-Run, ~5′)
Set 1 · 10 reps · No Ball1. Light Jog  2. Arm Circles  3. Arms Alternating  4. Body Twists  5. Knees Up  6. Heel Touches  7. Open the Gates  8. Close the Gates  9. Kick Out Sideways  10. Kick Out Diagonally
Set 2 · 5 reps · Technical Dribbling1. Drive, Step-Over, Pass  2. Drive, Feint, Pass (Left)  3. Drive, Feint, Pass (Right)  4. Drive, La Coquetta, Pass  5. Drive, Cruyff Turn, Pass
Part 2 — Throw-In Reps (~12′)
Two Facing Lines Per Grid — Continuous Shuttle Across

Rep 1 — Chest Control Shuttle

Thrower throws in to Target's chest. Target chests it down, one touch, and passes back to Thrower. Thrower dribbles the full width across to the opposite line and passes to the first person in that line. That player throws the ball in back to the Thrower, who chests it down, passes it back, and joins the back of the line.

Rep 2 — Check & Run, Long Throw

Target checks in toward the Thrower, then breaks away on a run while the ball is thrown long, over the shoulder, timed to the run. Target collects, drives, and passes across to the front of the opposite line. The Thrower then steps into the Target's spot, ready to check in and make that same run on the next rep.

Coach Note

Part 1 is routine by now, low intervention needed. Part 2 is a technical install, not a fitness rep, slow the group down if technique breaks under speed.

Coaching Points
  • Both feet down, equal release on the throw. No cheating technique even in warm-up
  • Chest control absorbs the ball, does not fight it
  • Check your shoulder before the long throw arrives so the turn is already decided
  • Every touch has a purpose, no standing over the ball

Objective Install three specific attacking corner patterns, replacing the improvised corners that cost us in the last two games.
Attacking Patterns — Walk-Through, No Opposition (15′, ~5′ each)
The Loop — Target Runner Loops Free at the Back Post
The LoopTarget Runner starts closest to the ball among the runners, with three decoys clustered between the Target and the back post. As the taker steps to strike, the Target spins away from the ball and loops around the outside of the cluster to arrive free at the back post. The decoys peel off toward the near post and center of goal.
The Bus Queue — Vertical Queue Peels Off, #4 Diagonal Decoy
The Bus QueueFour runners queue up in a single file from the edge of the box toward the penalty spot. On the taker's first step, a decoy makes a diagonal run toward goal to open space. The four queued runners peel off to different parts of the goal. The runner starting farthest back gets the longest run-up and hits the near post hardest, using that momentum.
Inward Loop (Short Corner) — Target Loops Outside the Decoy Cluster
Inward Loop (Short Corner)Three decoys sprint together toward the far post, dragging their markers and creating traffic. The Target loops around the outside of that traffic to arrive free near post. A blocker steps across the Target's marker to seal them off. Ball is played short, then along the ground for a first-time finish.
Defending Note (Secondary)

Quick reminder before live reps, not today's focus: full man-marking, every attacker gets a defender assigned before the serve, called out loud. GK organizes verbally, near-post duty folds into whichever matchup starts nearest that post. Just enough structure so live corners aren't chaos.

Coach Note

Assign roles based on who you've got. The Loop and Inward Loop's Target Runners want good spatial timing more than pure speed. The Bus Queue's back-of-queue runner wants your most explosive mover.

Coaching Points
  • Go last, arrive first. Let your teammates clear the path before you spin (The Loop)
  • Use the run-up. The farther back you start, the more speed you carry into the ball (Bus Queue)
  • Let the traffic build before you loop, arrive last, arrive free (Inward Loop)
  • This is the direct fix for the attacking patterns that went missing. Let the reps expose the gap first, then ask the question, don't lecture it
  • Defenders: know your mark before the serve, that's the only ask today

Half-Field Grid — Live Opposition, Own Half / Middle Third Free Kick
Objective Replace the automatic long boot with an actual read: play it quick while the opponent is scattered, or slow it down when the game state calls for it. This is the direct fix for giving the ball away for free right after winning it back.

Attacking Decision

Opponent still scattered, not reset: play forward immediately to a moving target. Opponent set, or we are up by 2+ goals: slow it down, go long, protect the result.

Coach Note

Let a few reps go wrong on purpose first so the cost of rushing it is visible before you correct it.

Coaching Points
  • Are they set or not? That decides quick vs. slow, not habit
  • Does rushing this actually gain us anything right now?
  • This is the direct fix for the ball we keep giving away for free right after winning it back

Full Field — Two Full-Size Goals, GKs Both Ends, vs. Strikers Blue
Objective Test the three corner routines and the free kick read live at full speed, with restarts manufactured to keep the reps coming, without being run from the sideline.

Format

Full field, full-size goals, GKs in both ends. All restarts are awarded as corner kicks. Coaches also call fouls anywhere on the field, however ridiculous, to manufacture free kick opportunities and keep the reps coming.

Coaching Approach

You're actively driving this one, not standing back. Call the fouls, award the corners, keep the whistle busy so reps stay high. Inside that, stay hands-off on execution: let the routine or the read play out on its own, freeze only on a genuinely bad rep to ask one sharp question, then restart immediately.

Coaching Points
  • Which routine did you run, and did everyone know their job in it?
  • Was that free kick read actually quick or slow, or a guess?
  • Who was your mark on the corner we just defended?
  • If it falls apart here, that's information for next week, not failure
